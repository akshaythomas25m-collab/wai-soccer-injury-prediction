The finding

The best feature contained the answer

Five features described each player's injury history, built by aggregating their whole Transfermarkt record. That record overlaps the seasons being predicted — so the aggregate averaged in the very injury the model was asked to classify.

The three models finish within 0.006 AUC of each other — statistically indistinguishable. Before the repair, XGBoost appeared to beat logistic regression by 2.5 points. Tree ensembles exploit leaked features more effectively than a linear model does, so removing the leak removed most of their apparent advantage. Phase 4 predicted logistic regression would place last on non-linearity grounds; it does, but by a margin too small to claim as a vindicated prediction.

Drivers

Diagnosis beats history

Mean absolute SHAP value, XGBoost on the corrected feature set. Before the repair this ranking was led by avg_days_missed at 0.69 — nearly seven times what it scores now.

What kind of injury it is predicts severity; who the player is barely does. The chi-square test agrees — injury category scores 2165.6 against severity, an order of magnitude above position (24.7) or league (235.7). For a medical department that argues for triage by diagnosis at the point of assessment, rather than flagging players with long injury records.

Method

How it was built

Each phase produced a check that constrained the next one.

  1. 01–02

    Merge and clean

    Primary injury records joined to Transfermarkt profiles on player name. Stripping the trailing numeric id from Transfermarkt names took the match rate from 0% to 97.2%.

  2. 03

    Exploratory analysis

    Fractures are the most severe category at 72.0% severe. The Premier League is the riskiest at 45.2%. Hamstring injuries are the most common single type at 1,256 cases.

  3. 04

    Assumptions and diagnostics

    Dropped days_numeric, games_missed and two target-encoded rate features as leakage. Dropped age_squared and total_days_missed for collinearity. Flagged that rows are not independent — 15,603 injuries across 4,081 players.

  4. 05

    Modelling

    Grouped split on player, StratifiedGroupKFold for tuning, and class weighting rather than SMOTE — at 1.8:1 the imbalance is mild enough that synthesising minority rows would add distortion without adding information.

  5. 05b

    Leakage repair

    History features rebuilt with an as-of join that admits only injuries starting strictly before the row being scored. 12.5% of rows have no prior injury on record and correctly receive zero — a genuine first-recorded-injury signal.

Open items

What still needs fixing

Stated here rather than left for a reader to find.

  • Residual leak

    One feature still looks ahead

    injuries_in_dataset counts a player's injuries across the whole 2020–25 window, so it uses rows from after the prediction point. It ranks third in the driver chart. Milder than what was repaired — it counts injuries without encoding their outcomes — but it should be made as-of or dropped.

  • Error in write-up

    The injury-history insight is stated backwards

    The Phase 3 summary reads "players with prior injuries show higher severe injury rates". The data says the opposite: median prior injuries are 10 for severe cases against 12 for non-severe, and the correlation is negative at −0.11. Players who accumulate many recorded injuries accumulate mostly minor ones.

  • Stale

    Threshold tuning predates the repair

    The decision-threshold sweep was run against the leaked model, where 0.49 was optimal for XGBoost. It needs re-running on the corrected model — and ideally on a validation fold rather than the test set, which makes the tuned figure mildly optimistic.

  • Scope

    Name matching is imperfect

    97.2% of rows link to a Transfermarkt id. The remaining 2.8% receive zeros for every history feature, which is indistinguishable from a genuine absence of prior injuries.
